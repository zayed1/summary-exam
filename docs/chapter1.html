1 The Meaning of Quality slide 4

Quality is multi-dimensional. The text gives the Eight Dimensions of Quality that describe quality in most industrial and many business situations:

#DimensionQuestion it answers
1PerformanceWill the product do the intended job?
2ReliabilityHow often does the product fail?
3DurabilityHow long does the product last?
4ServiceabilityHow easy is it to repair the product?
5AestheticsWhat does the product look like?
6FeaturesWhat does the product do?
7Perceived QualityWhat is the reputation of the company or its product?
8Conformance to StandardsIs the product made exactly as the designer intended?

2 Additional Dimensions for Service Organizations slide 5

In service and transactional business organizations (banking and finance, health care, customer service), three more dimensions are added:

  1. Responsiveness — How long did it take the service provider to reply to your request for service?
  2. Professionalism — The knowledge and skills of the service provider; the competency of the organization to provide the required services.
  3. Attentiveness — Customers want caring, personalized attention from their service providers.

3 Fitness for Use — the Traditional Definition slide 6

There are two general aspects of fitness for use:

① Quality of Design

The intentional differences in grade or quality level between products that serve the same function.

② Quality of Conformance

How well the product conforms to the specifications required by the design.

⚠ Note

This (quality = fitness for use) is the traditional definition of quality.

4 The Modern Definition of Quality slide 7

⭐ Modern Definition

“Quality is inversely proportional to variability.”

key relation Quality  ∝  1Variability

As variability in the important characteristics of a product decreases, the quality of the product increases. This is why reducing variability is the central goal of statistical quality control.

5 Statistical Methods slide 19

Three statistical methods are useful for quality improvement:

① Statistical Process Control (SPC)

  • Control charts, plus other problem-solving tools.
  • Useful in monitoring processes and reducing variability through elimination of assignable causes.
  • An on-line technique (in-process procedure).

② Designed Experiments (DOX)

  • Discovering the key factors that influence process performance.
  • Process optimization.
  • An off-line technique.

③ Acceptance Sampling

Inspection and classification of a sample of units selected at random, related to acceptance or rejection of the product.

💡 Quick contrast

SPC = on-line (monitoring during production)  ·  DOX = off-line (design / optimization).

6 The PDCA / PDSA Cycle slide 32

Deming recommended the Shewhart cycle as a model to guide improvement. Its four steps — Plan-Do-Check-Act — are called the PDCA cycle. When the Check step is called Study, it becomes the PDSA cycle.

StepMeaning
PlanPropose a change in the system aimed at improvement.
DoCarry out the change, usually on a small or pilot scale, to learn the results.
Check (Study)Analyze the results to determine what has been learned about the changes.
ActAdopt the change, or — if it was unsuccessful — abandon it.
⚠ Key point

The process is almost always iterative, and may require several cycles to solve complex problems.

★ Quick Review

  • 8 dimensions of quality + 3 service dimensions (Responsiveness, Professionalism, Attentiveness).
  • Fitness for use = Quality of design + Quality of conformance → traditional definition.
  • Modern definition: quality is inversely proportional to variability.
  • 3 statistical methods: SPC (on-line) · DOX (off-line) · Acceptance sampling.
  • Improvement cycle: PDCA / PDSA — iterative.
