1 Process Capability & Natural Tolerance Limits slides 3–4

Process capability analysis quantifies process variability and analyzes it relative to product specifications. Process capability refers to the uniformity of the process. Variability is viewed two ways:

  1. The natural / inherent variability at a specified time — “instantaneous” variability.
  2. The variability over time.

It is customary to take the Six-Sigma spread of the quality-characteristic distribution as the measure of process capability. For a normal distribution with mean μ and standard deviation σ, the natural tolerance limits are:

UNTL = μ + 3σ  ·  LNTL = μ − 3σ
Key facts

The natural tolerance limits contain 99.73% of the output; only 0.27% falls outside — which is 2,700 nonconforming parts per million (ppm). If the output is non-normal, the percentage outside μ ± 3σ may differ considerably from 0.27%.

2 Process Capability Ratio (Cp) slides 10–12

σ is almost always unknown and is replaced by an estimate σ̂ (from s or R̄/d₂). The process capability ratio (PCR) for two-sided specifications:

Cp Ĉp = USL − LSL6 σ̂

The PCR has a practical interpretation — the percentage of the specification band used up by the process:

P = 1Ĉp × 100

One-Sided PCRs slide 11

Cpu = USL − μ3σ  (upper only)  ·  Cpl = μ − LSL3σ  (lower only)
📊 Example — bursting strength (LSL = 200)

x̄ = 264, s = 32:   Cpl = (264 − 200) / (3 × 32) = 0.67.

Fallout: area left of Z = (200 − 264)/32 = −2 → about 2.28% defective ≈ 22,800 ppm.

Interpretation (Table 8.2)

For a normal, stable process: Cp = 1 → 2,700 ppm (two-sided); Cp = 1.5 → 4 ppm (one-sided). Higher Cp ⟹ fewer nonconforming parts.

3 Cpk — Actual Capability & Centering slides 15–16

⚠ Why Cpk?

Cp does not take process centering into account — it measures potential capability, not actual. Cpk takes centering into account and measures actual capability:

Cpk Cpk = min( Cpu , Cpl )
  • If Cpk = Cp → the process is centered at the midpoint of the specifications.
  • If Cpk < Cp → the process is off center.

4 Capability Analysis Using a Control Chart slides 19–21

Histograms, probability plots and PCRs summarize performance but don’t address statistical control. The control chart should be regarded as the primary technique of process capability analysis (both attributes and variables charts can be used).

📊 Example — bottle strength (Table 8.5, 20 samples of 5)

x̄ chart: CL = x̄ = 264.06; UCL = x̄ + A₂R̄ = 264.06 + (0.577)(77.3) = 308.66; LCL = 219.46.

R chart: CL = R̄ = 77.3; UCL = D₄R̄ = (2.115)(77.3) = 163.49; LCL = D₃R̄ = 0.

Estimate σ from the range: σ̂ = R̄/d₂ = 77.3 / 2.326 = 33.23. With LSL = 200: Cpl = (264.06 − 200)/(3 × 33.23) ≈ 0.64.

5 Gauge & Measurement Systems Capability slides 22–25

Observed variability comes partly from the units measured and partly from the measurement system (instrument/gauge, operator, conditions, calibration). Purpose of a measurement-systems capability study:

  1. Determine how much of the observed variability is due to the gauge.
  2. Isolate the components of variability in the measurement system.
  3. Assess whether the gauge is capable (suitable for its intended use).
The two R’s of measurement capability

Repeatability — same value when the same unit is measured several times under identical conditions.   Reproducibility — variation when measured under different conditions (operators, time…). Other aspects: linearity, stability, bias.

Basic MSA model — total observed measurement = true value + measurement error:

Eq 8.23 y = x + ε   ⟹   σ²Total = σ²P + σ²Gauge

6 The Precision-to-Tolerance (P/T) Ratio slides 28–29

Compares gauge capability to the tolerance band (USL − LSL):

P/T = k · σ̂GaugeUSL − LSL

Popular constants: k = 5.15 (95% tolerance interval covering ≥99% of a normal population) or k = 6 (the usual natural tolerance limits).

📊 Example

σ̂Gauge = 0.887, USL = 60, LSL = 5, k = 6:   P/T = 6(0.887)/(60 − 5) = 5.32/55 = 0.097.

P/T ≤ 0.1 is usually taken to imply adequate gauge capability (use with caution).

7 Variance Components, SNR & Discrimination Ratio slides 30–33

From a gauge study, estimate the total standard deviation, then separate the product and gauge parts:

σ̂²Total = s²  ·  σ̂²P = σ̂²Total − σ̂²Gauge
📊 Example 8.7 (continued)

s = √(392.4/39) = 3.17 → σ̂²Total = (3.17)² = 10.05; σ̂²P = (3.17)² − (0.887)² = 9.26; σ̂P = 3.04.

Ratios of variability slide 31

ρP = σ²Pσ²Total  (8.26)  ·  ρM = σ²Gaugeσ²Total  (8.27)  ·  ρP = 1 − ρM

Example: ρ̂M = 0.79/10.05 = 0.0786 → the instrument contributes ≈ 7.86% of total variance, so ρP = 0.9214.

Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) slide 32

Eq 8.28 SNR = √2 ρP1 − ρP

The number of distinct levels reliably obtained. AIAG: SNR ≥ 5 recommended; < 2 inadequate. Example: SNR = √(2·0.9214 / (1 − 0.9214)) = 4.84 → does not meet the SNR ≥ 5 guideline.

Discrimination Ratio (DR) slide 33

Eq 8.29 DR = 1 + ρP1 − ρP

For a capable gauge, DR > 4. Example: DR = (1 + 0.9214)/(1 − 0.9214) = 24.45 → the gauge is capable by this measure.

8 Accuracy, Precision & Gauge R&R slides 34–35

Accuracy vs. Precision

Accuracy — the ability of the instrument to measure the true value correctly on average (evaluated using a known standard; fixed by calibration). Precision — the inherent variability in the measurement system.

The gauge variance itself splits into two components — the basis of a gauge R & R study:

σ²Gauge = σ²Repeatability + σ²Reproducibility

Repeatability = inherent precision of the gauge itself; Reproducibility = variability due to different operators / conditions.

★ Quick Review

  • Capability = process uniformity; measured by the Six-Sigma spread; natural limits μ ± 3σ (2700 ppm out).
  • Cp = (USL−LSL)/6σ̂ → potential capability (ignores centering); Cpk = min(Cpu, Cpl) → actual capability. Cpk < Cp ⟹ off center.
  • The control chart is the primary technique of capability analysis; estimate σ̂ = R̄/d₂.
  • MSA: σ²Total = σ²P + σ²Gauge; gauge splits into repeatability + reproducibility (gauge R&R).
  • Gauge adequacy: P/T ≤ 0.1, SNR ≥ 5, DR > 4.
